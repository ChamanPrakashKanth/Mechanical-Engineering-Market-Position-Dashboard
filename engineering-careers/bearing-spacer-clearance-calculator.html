How do I calculate bearing spacer axial clearance?

For this simple one-dimensional chain, clearance equals the available housing length minus two bearing widths and the spacer length. Worst-case minimum clearance uses the smallest housing and largest components; maximum clearance uses the largest housing and smallest components.

Worked example

Two bearings of 10 ±0.02 mm and a 20 ±0.03 mm spacer occupy 40 ±0.07 mm. A housing of 40.2 ±0.04 mm gives nominal clearance 0.2 mm and worst-case clearance from 0.09 to 0.31 mm.

This model assumes both bearings have the same nominal width and tolerance. It adds their worst-case tolerances separately. It excludes shoulders, retaining rings, thermal expansion, elastic deformation, bearing internal clearance and preload. Add those requirements to your actual assembly review.

Formulas and assumptions

Annular area = π(OD² − bore²)/4. Volume = area × length. Convert mm³ to cm³ by dividing by 1000. Mass in grams = volume in cm³ × density in g/cm³. For the axial assembly, C = H − 2B − S and worst-case tolerance = T_H + 2T_B + T_S.

These are nominal educational geometry and dimension-chain tools. They do not certify strength, sealing, bearing preload, material selection or manufacturing suitability.
